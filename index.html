About Us

Who We Are

Nestled on 14 peaceful acres in Crossville, Tennessee, Dragon Haven is a private campground and sanctuary created for the pagan community. Founded by Jenn and Larry, who purchased the property in 2012, Dragon Haven was built with a dream of creating a welcoming place where people of all pagan paths could gather, celebrate, and connect with nature. After nearly a decade of dedication and hard work, that dream became a reality in the fall of 2021.

Today, our grounds feature a sacred Stone Circle, a fairy garden, ponds and waterfalls, nature trails, campsites, fire pits, gardens, and a shower house. During our events, guests can enjoy workshops, children's activities in the Dragon's Nest, a community kitchen, Artisan Alley, and seasonal celebrations honoring the Wheel of the Year.

More than anything, Dragon Haven is about community. We welcome practitioners of all pagan traditions, along with those who are simply curious to learn. Our gatherings are family-friendly, LGBTQIA+ affirming, and rooted in kindness, respect, and inclusion.

Whether you're joining us for your first sabbat celebration or returning to reconnect with friends, we hope you'll find what so many others have found at Dragon Haven—a place to celebrate, connect, and feel at home.

Our Community

Community is at the Heart of Everything We Do

We welcome Wiccans, Druids, Heathens, Norse, Celtic, eclectic practitioners, solitaries, seekers, and people from all pagan paths, as well as those who are simply curious to learn. Our family-friendly, LGBTQIA+ affirming gatherings are built on kindness, respect, and a shared love of nature and the Wheel of the Year. Whether you're attending your first pagan event or have been walking your path for years, you'll find a welcoming community where many arrive as guests but leave as friends—and often return as family. Here, you'll discover more than a place to camp or celebrate; you'll find a place to belong.

EVENTS

Celebrating the Wheel of the Year

Imbolc

A festival celebrating the beginning of spring and the return of light.

Ostara

A festival celebrating the spring equinox and the balance of light and dark.

Beltane

A festival celebrating the arrival of summer and the peak of fertility.

Litha

A festival celebrating the summer solstice and the peak of the sun's power.

Lammas

A festival celebrating the first harvest and the abundance of the earth.

Mabon

A festival celebrating the autumn equinox and the harvest season.

Samhain

A festival celebrating the end of the harvest season and honoring the ancestors.

Yule

A festival celebrating the winter solstice and the return of light.
